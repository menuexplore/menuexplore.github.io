What to Order at Shukette 2026: First-Time Guide to the Best Dishes

Shukette, chef Ayesha Nurdjaja's Levantine restaurant in Chelsea, New York, is built around shared plates, house-made breads, dips, vegetables and dishes cooked over charcoal — not a conventional appetizer, entree and dessert menu. The first visit becomes much clearer when you order in stages: bread and dips first, a few plates from The Shuk next, one dish from Al Ha'esh (the charcoal grill) and dessert at the end.

Start with one bread — Frena, a Moroccan-style bread finished with garlic and oregano, or Lafa, a grilled flatbread with ramp butter and sea salt — and one or two dips. Not Your Average Hummus, topped with marinated chickpeas and bang bang shata, is the most requested dip; Beet Tzatziki is the brighter second option. This is "rip and dip": warm bread torn by hand is the tool you use to eat much of the meal, and keeping a piece nearby lets you finish the sauces and juices still to come.

From The Shuk, the vegetable-led heart of the menu, pick two or three plates — Pea Party, Glazed Sweet Potatoes, Egyptian Spiced Grilled Wings or Shrimp a la Plancha with harissa butter. From the charcoal grill, the saffron-yogurt-marinated Joojeh Chicken feeds two comfortably as part of the shared order; the Grass-Fed Steak Shish Kebab is the beef alternative. Finish with The Mic Drop, tahini soft serve with halva floss, hazelnut and pomegranate, shared by two or three — or the lighter Pomegranate Crasher sorbet. Shukette's menu lists no prices online, so check the current menu or ask your server for today's prices before you order. Guide last updated November 12, 2026.

Key facts

  • Best way to begin: one bread with one or two dips; signature dip is Not Your Average Hummus.
  • Best first charcoal-grilled dish: Joojeh Chicken — saffron-yogurt-marinated half chicken with toum, feeds two comfortably as part of the shared order.
  • Dessert not to skip: The Mic Drop — tahini soft serve with halva floss, hazelnut and pomegranate, shared by 2–3 people.
  • Ordering style: shared plates for the table; the meal is ordered in stages (bread and dips, The Shuk, Al Ha'esh, dessert).
  • Prices: Shukette's menu lists no prices online — check the restaurant's current menu or ask your server before ordering. Guide last updated November 12, 2026.
