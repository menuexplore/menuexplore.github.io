Shukette vs Shuka 2026: Chelsea vs SoHo — Which Should You Pick?

Shukette vs Shuka is the comparison almost every New York diner ends up making: two restaurants, one chef, and names so similar that first-timers regularly book the wrong one. Both belong to chef Ayesha Nurdjaja, both serve shared plates built around bread and bold Mediterranean flavors, and both are hard reservations. But they are not the same menu in two locations.

Shuka, the SoHo original, opened in 2017 and roams broadly across the Eastern Mediterranean, with Italian, Moroccan and Tunisian influences, in a calmer, traditional sit-down room built for conversation. Shukette, its Chelsea younger sibling, opened in 2021 — "Shukette" translates loosely as "little Shuka" — and narrows the lens to the Levant: Israeli, Syrian and Lebanese cooking built around a charcoal grill and a 23-seat counter in a loud, high-energy open kitchen. Shukette landed on The New York Times' list of the best new restaurants of 2021.

At both restaurants bread is central, not a prelude, and the meal is shared plates rather than a rigid appetizer-and-entree structure. Shukette's clearest difference is its shape: the Shuk, a vegetable-forward run of seasonal small plates named for the Hebrew and Arabic word for market; Al Ha'esh, "from the fire," everything cooked over natural hardwood charcoal — saffron-yogurt Joojeh Chicken with toum, the whole porgy of the Fish in a Cage, the Grass-Fed Steak Shish Kebab with carrot harissa; and the rip-and-dip ritual, house breads like Frena and Lafa torn by hand and dragged through dips such as the Not Your Average Hummus. The meal finishes with The Mic Drop, tahini soft serve with halva floss, hazelnut and pomegranate.

Pick by mood, not by hype: Shukette for a lively night out, a group dinner or a walk-in at the counter — a portion of the 23 seats is held for walk-ins, with the best odds at the 5pm opening or after 9:30pm — and Shuka for a quieter date or a dinner where conversation matters. Shukette is at 230 9th Ave, Chelsea, and serves dinner only, Monday to Saturday 5-11pm and Sunday 4-10pm; book tables through Resy, or call (212) 242-1803. Key facts

  • Shuka (SoHo) opened in 2017; Shukette (Chelsea) opened in 2021 — sister restaurants from chef Ayesha Nurdjaja; "Shukette" means roughly "little Shuka."
  • Shuka: broad Eastern Mediterranean tour, calmer sit-down room. Shukette: Levantine focus, charcoal grill, loud open kitchen with a 23-seat counter.
  • Shukette's format: the Shuk (vegetable-first small plates), Al Ha'esh ("from the fire" charcoal grill), and rip-and-dip breads Frena and Lafa with Not Your Average Hummus.
